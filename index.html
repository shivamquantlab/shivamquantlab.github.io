Watch sample means become more predictable.

Draw repeated random samples from a selected population, calculate each sample mean, and compare the resulting histogram with the normal approximation predicted by the Central Limit Theorem.

SE = σ / √n

Each population is parameterized to have a known mean and standard deviation.

n = 20

Number of observations in each sample.

1,500

More repetitions make the histogram smoother.

INTERPRETATIONThe CLT describes the distribution of sample means, not the raw population. As n increases, the normal approximation often improves while the standard error decreases. The rate and conditions depend on the population.

Estimate π using random points.

Sample points uniformly from a unit square and count how many fall inside the quarter circle. The area ratio gives an estimate of π. More points generally reduce sampling noise.

π ≈ 4 × (inside / total)
5,000

The simulation counts every point; the plot displays a representative subset for smooth rendering.

A quarter circle of radius 1 is inscribed in a unit square. Its area is π/4, while the square's area is 1.

MONTE CARLO POINT CLOUD5,000 trials
Inside quarter circle Outside quarter circle

—Total trials
INTERPRETATIONThis is a stochastic estimator, not an exact calculation. A single run can over- or under-estimate π. Compare runs at different sample sizes; the error does not have to decrease monotonically every time.
